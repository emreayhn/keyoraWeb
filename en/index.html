Your passwords stay
on your computer.

Keyora is a free, open-source password manager for Windows that works completely offline. No cloud, no account, no subscription. Your entries are stored encrypted, in a single file on your own machine.

Everything you expect from a password manager, without the cloud

Keyora never connects to the internet. No update checks, no usage statistics, no crash reports.

Fully offline

Your vault is a single SQLite file on your computer that holds your encrypted entries. It isn't tied to any server, account or internet connection.

Strong encryption

The key is derived from your master password with Argon2id. Passwords, app names, usernames and notes are all encrypted with Fernet.

Locks when Windows locks

Win+L, sleep or a dropped remote desktop session lock the vault immediately. It also locks itself when you're idle.

Clipboard cleared in 20 seconds

Copied passwords are wiped from the clipboard after 20 seconds and never land in Windows clipboard history (Win+V) or cloud clipboard.

Automatic encrypted backups

An encrypted backup is made once a day when you open the vault, and when you lock or close it after making changes. The last 10 are kept by default, in a folder you choose.

Hidden from screenshots

Screen sharing, screenshot and recording tools can't see the Keyora window, so your passwords stay private even when you share your screen in a meeting. On by default (before Windows 10 2004 the window shows up black instead).

Password history

The last 5 passwords of every entry are kept, so you can recover one you changed by mistake.

Strong password generator

Pick the length and character types; passwords come from the operating system's secure random number generator.

Dark and light themes

Switch instantly between pastel dark and light themes in Settings, and keep your hands on the keyboard with shortcuts.

See which passwords need changing

The Password Health report scans your vault and lists the entries that need attention. It's calculated entirely on your computer.

  • Weak passwords: short or using too few character types
  • Reused passwords: the same password on more than one account
  • Old passwords: not changed for a year

Move your passwords over in minutes

Pick the export file from the app you use now. Keyora detects the format, shows a preview first, skips duplicates and makes a backup before importing.

  • Chrome, Edge and Brave
  • Firefox
  • Bitwarden
  • KeePassXC and KeePass 2
  • LastPass
  • Keyora CSV

How is it protected?

Keyora doesn't invent its own cryptography; it relies on widely used, well-reviewed libraries.

Master password

Never stored anywhere. Only you know it.

Two separate Argon2id runs

One for verification, one for the encryption key, each with its own random salt (64 MB memory, 3 passes).

Fernet encryption

AES-128-CBC with HMAC-SHA256. Keyora never writes the key to disk; it is held in memory only while the vault is open.

Brute-force protection

After 3 wrong attempts, the wait time grows with each try. Restarting the app doesn't reset it.

Important: If you forget your master password, your vault cannot be opened in any way. There is no recovery email and no back door; that's by design. Keep your master password somewhere safe.

Frequently asked questions

Have another question? Ask on GitHub.

Where is my data stored?

Your vault lives in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Keyora\keyora.db, inside your Windows user folder. Wherever you install the app, it uses the same file. Your entries are encrypted and can't be read without your master password.

Is there cloud sync or a phone app?

No, on purpose. The point of Keyora is that your passwords never leave your computer. To move to another computer, carry a backup file over yourself: install Keyora there, create a temporary vault, then restore the backup from Ayarlar → Yedekleme → Yedekten Geri Yükle (Settings → Backup → Restore from Backup). The restored vault opens with the master password it had when the backup was made.

Windows warns about an "unknown publisher". Is it safe?

The warning appears because the file isn't signed with a paid code-signing certificate; it says nothing about the app itself. Choose "More info → Run anyway" to continue. The full source code is public on GitHub.

Will uninstalling delete my passwords?

No. Uninstalling removes only the program; your vault and backups stay where they are, and reinstalling opens the same vault. To remove everything, delete the %LOCALAPPDATA%\Keyora folder yourself. If you chose a different backup folder in Settings, delete that too.

I use Keyora 1.0.0. How do I upgrade?

Just run the new installer; your vault is found automatically. On first sign-in it's converted to the new format, and a backup is made automatically before the conversion. Afterwards, delete any old 1.0.0 exe copies, since the old version can't read the converted vault correctly.

Can I store two-factor (2FA) codes?

No. Keeping passwords and 2FA codes in the same place defeats the purpose of the second factor. That's why 2FA codes are deliberately left out when importing, and Keyora tells you how many were skipped.

Does it cost anything? Any ads or data collection?

Keyora is free and open source under the MIT license. It has no ads, collects no data and never goes online. This page has no ads or visitor-tracking scripts either.
