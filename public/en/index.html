Where are your passwords?

C:\Users\you\AppData\Local\Keyora\keyora.db

With Keyora, the answer is short: on your own computer, in this file. Your entries are encrypted, and only the master password you know can open them. No cloud, no account, no subscription; Keyora never connects to the internet.

Everything you expect from a password manager. Except the cloud.

A simple, keyboard-friendly interface. The screens below come from a test vault filled with made-up entries.

  1. Unlock your vault

    Only your master password opens the vault. After 3 wrong attempts, the wait grows with each try (up to 5 minutes); restarting the app doesn't reset it.

  2. Find what you need

    Search by app name, username or category; the list filters as you type. Passwords are masked in the list, and the eye icon shows one for 10 seconds.

  3. Copy with one click

    Copy a username or password. Whatever you copy is wiped from the clipboard after 20 seconds and never lands in Win+V history or cloud clipboard.

  4. Spot weak passwords

    The Password Health report lists passwords that are weak (short or using too few character types), reused across accounts, or unchanged for a year. It's calculated entirely on your computer.

  5. Bring your passwords over

    Pick the export file from Chrome, Edge, Brave, Firefox, Bitwarden, KeePassXC, KeePass 2 or LastPass. Keyora shows a preview first, skips duplicates and makes a backup before importing. Two-factor (2FA) codes are deliberately left out.

  6. Lock it and walk away

    Press Ctrl+L or the Lock button. When Windows locks (Win+L), goes to sleep or a remote desktop session drops, Keyora locks too; it also locks itself when you're idle.

How is your key made?

Keyora doesn't invent its own cryptography; it relies on widely used, well-reviewed libraries. Two separate things are derived from your master password.

  • master passwordNever stored anywhere.
    • Argon2id, salt 1 → verification hashOnly answers "is the password right?"
    • Argon2id, salt 2 → encryption key64 MB memory, 3 passes. Keyora doesn't write the key to disk.
      • Fernet → encrypted entriesAES-128-CBC and HMAC-SHA256.

What's encrypted?

Passwords, app names, usernames, notes and category names. The contents can't be read without your master password. Some details, such as the number of entries and dates, are not encrypted in the file.

Questions, answered

Where is my data stored?

Your vault lives in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Keyora\keyora.db, inside your Windows user folder. Wherever you install the app, it uses the same file. Your entries are encrypted and can't be read without your master password.

Is there cloud sync or a phone app?

No, on purpose. The point of Keyora is that your passwords never leave your computer. To move to another computer, carry a backup file over yourself: install Keyora there, create a temporary vault, then restore the backup from Ayarlar → Yedekleme → Yedekten Geri Yükle (Settings → Backup → Restore from Backup). The restored vault opens with the master password it had when the backup was made.

Windows warns about an "unknown publisher". Is it safe?

The warning appears because the file isn't signed with a paid code-signing certificate; it says nothing about the app itself. Choose "More info", then "Run anyway" to continue. The full source code is public on GitHub.

Will uninstalling delete my passwords?

No. Uninstalling removes only the program; your vault and backups stay where they are, and reinstalling opens the same vault. To remove everything, delete the %LOCALAPPDATA%\Keyora folder yourself. If you chose a different backup folder in Settings, delete that too.

I use Keyora 1.0.0. How do I upgrade?

Just run the new installer; your vault is found automatically. On first sign-in it's converted to the new format, and a backup is made automatically before the conversion. Afterwards, delete any old 1.0.0 exe copies, since the old version can't read the converted vault correctly. The pre-conversion backup is in the old format, so its app names, usernames, notes and category names are in plain text; once you're happy with the new version, delete it from Ayarlar → Yedekleme → Eski Yedeği Sil (Settings → Backup → Delete Old Backup).

Can I store two-factor (2FA) codes?

No. Keeping passwords and 2FA codes in the same place defeats the purpose of the second factor. That's why 2FA codes are deliberately left out when importing, and Keyora tells you how many were skipped.

Does it cost anything? Any ads or data collection?

Keyora is free and open source under the MIT license. It has no ads, collects no data and never goes online. This page has no ads or visitor-tracking scripts either.
